Rich countries tax more and charge more, and subsidies sit with the rest

Three visuals test the link between income, tax rates, prices and subsidies.

Viz 7 · Bars by income level

High-income countries tax at 35% and pay 3.19 USD per litre

Low-income countries tax slightly more (22.9%) than middle-income countries (21.1%), yet pay less at the pump.

Viz 8 · Scatter plot with trend line

Each extra point of tax goes with about 10 cents more per litre

The relationship is statistically significant. Countries fall into two clusters, around 15% tax and around 35 to 40%, so the line mostly describes the gap between the groups. It shows association and does not show that tax causes higher prices.

Method

How it was built

Data

One CSV of weekly prices from January 2020, pivoted so diesel, LPG and petrol each have their own row. Fields cover country, region, income level, subsidy level, Brent crude, tax % and price.

Date fix

Dates arrived as text and were switched to a Date type in the Data Source tab, which made the monthly trend and forecast possible.

Calculated fields

Pre-Tax Price, Tax Amount = Price − Pre-Tax Price and Volatility (CV) = STDEV(Price) / AVG(Price).

Dashboard

Nine sheets in three rows, one per reader, with a shared Fuel Type filter and a clean set of legends.
